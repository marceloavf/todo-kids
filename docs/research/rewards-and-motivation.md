# Rewards and motivation

The product question is how a guardian can help a child practice a household routine without turning it into a contest. The research review drew on studies of rewards, descriptive praise, autonomy and visual routines. Much of that work concerns classrooms, laboratories or clinical settings. It does not validate this specific app or establish a universal points schedule.

| Design decision | What to look for in the family pilot |
| --- | --- |
| Let a task award zero points. | Can the family gradually remove points when a routine becomes familiar? |
| Keep balances individual and omit sibling rankings. | Does the child understand their own progress without comparing siblings? |
| Give a short, actionable hint on rejection. | Can the child explain what to try next? |
| Encourage specific feedback about the action. | Does the guardian describe what they saw when approving the task? |
| Let the family choose rewards and discuss adjustments. | Does the child understand what was agreed before points change? |

Research on external rewards differs by study design, age, task and the kind of reward offered. The original review distinguishes full texts from abstracts, grades the strength of findings, and separates product inferences from research results. Points, reminders and celebrations are family choices; time in the app is not a success measure.

Sources: [Deci, Koestner and Ryan on extrinsic rewards](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627), [Cameron, Banko and Pierce on reward effects](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF03392017), [original evidence tables, limitations and bibliography](https://github.com/marceloavf/todo-kids/blob/f854579/docs/research/boas-praticas-psicologia.md).
